How to play

Partners sit opposite. Card order in each suit: J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7. Points: J 3, 9 2, A 1, 10 1. A deal has 28 points.

Bid 16–28 on your first 4 cards. Then the opponents may double the stakes (×2) and the bidder may redouble (×4). Single Hand: after trump is set and before the first lead, any player may declare it. They play alone, their partner sits out, and they must win all 8 tricks: +3 game points if they do, −3 if they lose any trick. The bidding team’s pips move one step per game point: 1, 2 or 4 steps at stakes ×1, ×2 or ×4.

The bidder picks a suit to set as trump — it does not have to be a card in their hand — and trump stays hidden. Two other choices: Joker means no trump this deal. 7th card means the suit of the bidder’s 7th card is trump; the bidder sees it but cannot play it until someone asks for trump or it is the last trick. Everyone then gets 4 more cards.

Follow the lead suit. If you cannot, you may ask for trump before you play. Once you play your card you cannot ask on that turn. After trump is asked, you must play a trump card if you hold one. The highest trump wins, otherwise the highest card of the lead suit. If trump is revealed during a trick, every trump-suit card in that trick counts as trump, even one played before the reveal.

Royals: K and Q of trump, once your team has won a trick. The bid drops by 4 for the bidding team, or rises by 4 if the opponents declare. Match scoring uses six pips per team. A made bid turns a black pip off, or else an off pip red. A failed bid turns a red pip off, or else an off pip black. Only the bidding team’s pips change. Six red pips (+6) win the match; six black pips (−6) lose it.

Online: all cards are stored in the shared table data, so use it for friendly games.
